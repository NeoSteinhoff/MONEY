## SECTION 1 · THE THESIS

Padel is the most-played sport in the UAE and there is no UAE-born brand dressing the people who play it. The country has 950+ padel courts across 320+ venues as of April 2026, with 400+ courts in Dubai alone [LIVE DATA — Padel Gameplan UAE Padel Statistics 2026, surfaced via search August 2026]. Every dirham of that apparel spend currently leaves the country: it goes to Bullpadel, NOX, adidas, Head and Joma — Spanish and European performance brands whose visual language is neon, foil-print, compression-fit and aggressively technical. The UAE padel player is not a Spanish professional. He or she is a 27–42 year-old Dubai professional paying AED 247/hour at median peak court rates [LIVE DATA — Playtomic live data March 2026 via Padel Gameplan], playing three times a week, and then going straight to brunch. That person wants court-to-street, not court-to-podium. Neo's 60-person promoter network is the exact distribution asset this requires, because padel in Dubai is not a sport — it is the social calendar, and the clubs (Matcha Club, Padel Pro, Just Padel) are the new venues. BANDEJA sells a heavyweight garment-dyed court polo at AED 285, a washed club cap at AED 165, and a woven court towel at AED 95, made in Dubai in numbered drops of 160, seeded courtside before it is ever advertised.

### QC Inspection Checklist — 8 Points, run before accepting any batch

1. **GSM verification.** Weigh a garment on a kitchen scale, divide by the garment's surface area, or send one unit to a testing lab for the first batch. If the spec says 240gsm and the batch is 195gsm, the factory substituted. Reject.
2. **Colour consistency across the run.** Lay 10 units from different cartons side by side under daylight. Garment dye batches drift. Any visible two-tone difference between units = reject that carton.
3. **Embroidery registration and density.** Check 10 units: logo position within ±3mm of spec, no thread pull, no puckering of the fabric behind the stitch, backing fully trimmed.
4. **Neck label.** Your woven label present, factory label fully removed (no residual stitching), care and composition label legible and **including the Arabic-language elements required for UAE retail** (see Section 5).
5. **Seam integrity.** Pull the shoulder seam and side seam of 5 units firmly. Any thread slip = full-batch reject. Check stitch density: 10–12 stitches per inch minimum.
6. **Collar construction.** The single most visible failure point in a polo. Roll the collar of 10 units, release, and check it returns flat. A collapsing collar destroys a AED 285 price point instantly.
7. **Sizing verification.** Measure chest, body length and sleeve on 2 units per size against the spec sheet. Tolerance ±1cm. Sizing drift is the #1 driver of apparel returns and returns are the #1 killer of first-time apparel operators' cash.
8. **Packing and presentation.** Every unit individually bagged, folded to spec, hangtag attached at the same point, no loose threads. Open 10 units as if you were the customer. If the unboxing doesn't hold up, fix it before the first order ships, not after the first bad review.

### Negative Space — 3 Things BANDEJA Is NOT

1. **NOT a performance brand.** BANDEJA will never claim a compression technology, a moisture-wicking trademark, or a percentage improvement in anything. The moment it competes with NOX on spec, it loses, because NOX is genuinely better at spec. It competes on the two hours *around* the match, not the match.
2. **NOT loud.** No large chest logos, no gradients, no foil, no all-over prints, no sponsor-block layouts, no neon. If a garment can be identified as BANDEJA from 20 metres away, it has failed.
3. **NOT a general activewear brand.** BANDEJA does not make leggings, gym shorts, running kit or hoodies-because-they-sell. It is padel, for a minimum of 24 months. The moment it becomes "activewear", it is competing with 8,000 UAE apparel stores instead of zero UAE padel apparel brands.

### Bilingual / Arabic Packaging Ruling

**Arabic on BANDEJA labelling and packaging: REQUIRED — YES.**

UAE labelling rules require Arabic as the official label language; Arabic-only or Arabic/English bilingual labels are accepted, and Arabic stickers are an accepted method of compliance [LIVE DATA — UAE labelling/marking requirements, trade.gov / UAE product registration guidance, surfaced Aug 2026]. Consumer product labels are required to carry the product name, country of origin, and manufacturer details, with enforcement by the standardisation authority (ESMA, now within MoIAT), which has powers to prohibit imports, suspend circulation and impose administrative sanctions [LIVE DATA — same sources]. Separately, textile products fall under a dedicated **UAE Scheme for the Control of Textile Products**, a Cabinet Resolution regime that mandates specific labelling, testing and certification requirements for textiles including fibre composition and flammability, with registration through approved conformity assessment bodies [LIVE DATA — Cabinet Resolution on the UAE Scheme for the Control of Textile Products, surfaced Aug 2026].

**Practical minimum for BANDEJA, on the sewn-in care label:**
- Fibre composition in Arabic and English (e.g. ٪٩٥ قطن ٪٥ إيلاستين / 95% Cotton 5% Elastane)
- Country of origin in Arabic and English (صُنع في الإمارات / Made in UAE — or صُنع في الصين / Made in China for Drop 002, and **do not obscure this**; it is both a legal requirement and a trust issue)
- Care symbols plus Arabic care text
- Importer/supplier name and UAE contact

**Cost:** certified Arabic translation of label and care copy at a Dubai legal translation office: **AED 150–400** [BENCHMARKED]. Woven care labels with bilingual text: add ~AED 1.20/unit at 500 units [ESTIMATED].

**Action:** Because the Textile Products Scheme carries registration and conformity requirements that a first-time operator will not intuit, **book a 30-minute paid consultation with a UAE product-registration consultancy in Week 2** (AED 300–600) before committing to Drop 002's import. Do not discover this at customs. [BENCHMARKED — consultancy rates; the requirement itself is LIVE DATA]

### UAE Business Setup

#### The minor-founder ruling — read this before anything else

Neo is 17. This is a real, specific legal constraint that most templated UAE setup advice ignores.

**The position [LIVE DATA — Khaleej Times, Gulf News and UAE legal commentary on the revised Commercial Transactions Law and the lowered age of majority, surfaced Aug 2026]:**
- The UAE has set **18 as the age of majority**, and at 18 a resident can apply for a trade licence, sign commercial contracts and execute legal documents **without a guardian**. The Ministry of Economy set **18 as the standard minimum age for a business**, and this applies to free zone setups as well.
- **However**, UAE law expressly contemplates minors in trade: a minor **may engage in trade from age 15**, whether under guardianship or custody, subject to rules and conditions issued by the Minister of Economy. A minor aged 15+ may **apply to a judge for permission to manage their own financial assets, including business interests**, where the court considers it in their best interests.
- For a 15–17 year old, **a guardian's consent and signature is required to open and operate a bank account.**

**The three practical routes, in order of preference:**

1. **Guardian-held licence with a written shareholders' agreement and transfer at 18.** The licence and the bank account are issued to Neo's parent/guardian; a signed agreement records Neo as beneficial owner and operator; the licence transfers on his 18th birthday. Fastest, cheapest, lowest friction with banks and with Tabby/Tamara underwriting. **This is the recommended route.**
2. **Court authorisation route.** Apply under the minor-trading provision for judicial permission. Legitimate, and a genuinely strong founder story, but it introduces a court timeline that is incompatible with a 30-day launch. Run it in parallel if desired; do not gate the launch on it.
3. **Wait until 18.** Only if 1 and 2 both fail. The Q4 window (DFC + Premier Padel P1) does not wait.

**Action:** confirm the position **in writing** with each freezone on Day 1, and with the bank on Day 3. Freezone policies on under-18 shareholders differ and none of this should be taken from a report — including this one — without written confirmation from the licensing authority itself. ### UAE Payment Gateway Ruling

- **Tabby — ENABLE, priority 1.** BNPL is ~14% of UAE ecommerce checkout by value and **18–27% of orders by volume on UAE Shopify stores, with AOV 22% higher than card-only checkouts** [LIVE DATA, 2026]. At AED 285, Tabby converts the objection "AED 285 for a polo" into "AED 71 now", which is a completely different psychological transaction.
- **Tamara — ENABLE, priority 2.** Tabby and Tamara jointly process ~USD 6B annualised UAE volume across ~30,000 merchants [LIVE DATA]. Customers are loyal to one or the other. Running both costs nothing incremental and captures both.
- **Apple Pay — ENABLE, mandatory.** UAE iPhone penetration is among the highest globally and Apple Pay removes the card-entry step that kills mobile conversion. Non-negotiable.
- **Cards via Shopify Payments (or Amazon Payment Services/PayFort as fallback)** — cards lead UAE spend at **49% share** [LIVE DATA — KPMG 2026].
- **COD — ENABLE, but with an AED 15 surcharge and an order cap.** COD is **25–30% of UAE transactions in 2026, down from 40% in 2022** [LIVE DATA — KPMG]. **Ruling: enable it.** Refusing COD in the UAE forfeits roughly a quarter of the addressable market, including exactly the older/less-digital segment that a Spanish import brand cannot reach. But COD carries real costs — failed deliveries, refusal at the door, cash handling, delayed settlement. The AED 15 surcharge (waived above AED 500) filters low-intent COD orders without excluding genuine ones. Cap COD at AED 600 per order and disable it entirely for first-time customers ordering 3+ units, which is the classic UAE COD fraud pattern.

### What Kills This in 30 Days — 3 Failure Scenarios
