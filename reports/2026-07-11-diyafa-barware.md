### QC Inspection Checklist (8 Points — Run Before Accepting Any Production Batch)
1. **Glass integrity:** Hold each piece to light — check for air bubbles, seam lines, or stress cracks at the stem join
2. **Weight consistency:** Weigh 10 random units per colourway; reject any batch with >5% variance from the approved sample weight
3. **Colour match:** Compare 10 random units against the approved sample under both daylight and warm indoor light — reject any batch with visible colour-family drift
4. **Rim finish:** Run a finger along the rim of 20 random units — any sharp or uneven edge is an automatic reject for that unit
5. **Stem/base stability:** Stand each of 20 random units on a flat, level surface — any that wobble or tip are rejected
6. **Brass tool finish:** Inspect 15 random bar-tool units under bright light for uneven plating, tarnish spots, or seam burrs
7. **Box fit:** Each glass must seat snugly in its foam divider with zero rattle when the box is gently shaken
8. **Drop-survival spot test:** Drop one unit per 20-unit batch from table height (approx. 75cm) onto a padded surface — document breakage rate; reject the batch if breakage exceeds 1 in 20 on this stress test

- Why it fits: Names the exact cultural ritual the product serves, not just the product category; works bilingually (دِيافة / DIYAFA) with a clean Latin transliteration; carries genuine gravitas without being a religious term, so it is safe across a broad multi-faith UAE customer base
- Domain status: diyafa.com — [NOT CHECKED, likely premium-priced if available]; **shopdiyafa.com**, **diyafa.co**, **diyafahome.com** — likely available [ASSUMED — verify via Namecheap before launch]
- Instagram handle: @diyafa likely taken or squatted (a well-known Dubai street, "Al Diyafah," shares the root word); @diyafa.co, @shopdiyafa, @diyafahome likely available [ASSUMED]

**Option 2: RIWAQ (رواق)**
- Etymology: A covered gallery, arcade, or veranda — architecturally, the transitional gathering space of a traditional Gulf home where guests are received before entering the majlis
- Why it fits: Evokes the physical *space* of hosting rather than the act itself; strong architectural/interiors resonance
- Domain: **riwaqhome.com**, **theriwaq.com** — likely available [ASSUMED]
- IG: @riwaqhome likely available [ASSUMED]

**Option 3: ANIQ (أنيق)**
- Etymology: Arabic for "elegant," "refined"
- Why it fits: Direct, descriptive, easy global pronunciation
- Domain: **aniqhome.com** — likely available [ASSUMED]
- IG: @aniq.home likely available [ASSUMED]
- Weakness: purely descriptive (elegant) rather than narrative — lower long-term brand-story depth than DIYAFA or RIWAQ

**Recommendation: DIYAFA**
Decision reasoning: DIYAFA is the only option that names the cultural *ritual* rather than the aesthetic or the space — it gives the brand a story to tell in every piece of content ("what does diyafa mean, and why did we build a brand around it") that ANIQ cannot generate and that RIWAQ only partially generates. The shared root with Dubai's well-known Al Diyafah Street is a minor naming-collision risk but functions more as free local recognition than confusion, since the brand and the street occupy entirely different commercial categories.

### Bilingual/Arabic Packaging Ruling
**Arabic on packaging: REQUIRED — YES.**
UAE Federal Law No. 15 of 2020 (Consumer Protection Law) and Cabinet Resolution No. 66 of 2023 require Arabic-language labelling on consumer goods sold in the UAE — this applies broadly to consumer products, not only food and cosmetics, and covers product name, country of origin, and material composition (relevant for glass/metal composition disclosure). ESMA (Emirates Authority for Standardization and Metrology) additionally governs general product labelling standards. At minimum: product name in Arabic (دِيافة), country of manufacture (صُنع في تركيا — "Made in Turkey," or صُنع في الإمارات for any UAE-finished component), and material composition on the insert card or box base.
