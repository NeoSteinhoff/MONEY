### QC Inspection Checklist (8 Points)
1. **Plating colour consistency:** compare 10 random units against the approved sample swatch under daylight-balanced light; reject any unit with a visibly different gold tone
2. **Plating thickness spot-check:** where a supplier provides XRF (ASTM B568) documentation, confirm ≥0.5 microns; without in-house XRF access, rely on the supplier's written spec plus the 7-day wear test
3. **Nickel test:** DMG swab test on 5 random units per batch — any pink reaction is an automatic batch reject
4. **Clasp integrity:** pull-test 20 units by hand; any clasp that loosens or opens under moderate force is rejected
5. **Weld/join inspection:** check chain links and jump rings under magnification for incomplete welds
6. **Waterproof claim validation:** submerge 3 units in water for 24 hours, then re-inspect for colour change or pitting
7. **Packaging fit:** each piece must sit securely in its branded box with no rattle in transit
8. **Batch consistency:** weigh 10 random units against spec weight; reject batch if >5% variance

### What Kills This in 30 Days — 3 Failure Scenarios

1. **Plating fails the durability claim it's built on.** If the first production batch tarnishes or fades within 30 days of the 8-point QC checklist's wear test, the entire brand thesis (durability none of the competitors credibly claim) collapses. **Early warning signal:** if any of the 9 sample pieces show visible colour change in the 7-day pool/sweat test in Section 4. **Action:** do not proceed to the 200-unit production order; switch suppliers immediately and re-test before committing capital.

2. **The Advertiser Permit regulation blocks the promoter-network channel.** If promoter-network members are unwilling or unable to obtain the UAE Media Council Advertiser Permit before Day 30, the core distribution advantage this thesis depends on is neutralised, leaving SAMAR as just another paid-social jewelry brand. **Early warning signal:** if fewer than 5 of the first 15 promoter contacts have confirmed permit status or willingness to apply by Day 10. **Action:** restructure the model so promoters simply supply the venue access and UGC footage, which SAMAR reposts from its own (permitted) brand account, rather than requiring native posts from unpermitted personal accounts.

3. **Standard Aramex/Emirates Post terms don't actually cover a lost or damaged jewelry shipment.** Both carriers list jewelry and precious metals as excluded from standard carriage liability, and Aramex's own upgrade insurance product is reported to exclude jewelry as well (see Section 4). If this isn't resolved in writing before the first batch ships, a single lost high-value order could wipe out a meaningful share of Month 1 margin with no recourse. **Early warning signal:** if Aramex's SME team cannot confirm a written declared-value arrangement for PVD-plated jewelry by Day 5. **Action:** escalate to a specialized insured-shipping provider (a Claisy-type service was identified charging ~0.75% of declared value with no minimum) rather than shipping uninsured.

4. **The UAE-native competitor cluster turns out to already own the promoter/nightlife channel.** If a manual audit (recommended Day 1–2, not yet done this session) reveals that Yumeira, Aglaiia, or another incumbent already runs an event/promoter-based distribution model, the white-space claim in Section 3 weakens substantially. **Early warning signal:** any of the 6 incumbents' Instagram grids showing venue/event UGC content at scale. **Action:** if found, pivot the differentiation further — toward the specific occasion-drop cadence and scarcity model, which is harder to copy than distribution channel alone.
